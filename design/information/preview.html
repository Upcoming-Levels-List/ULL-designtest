Design proposal

One page, four ways to enter it

/information holds five unrelated things — what the list is, how to read it, the guidelines, two legends, and who to talk to — and today it stacks them as cards of roughly equal size. The Pending List legend, nine rows of icons, is given a full-width card; the guidelines, thirteen sections across five groups, are given a 600 px window to be read through. Weight on the page is currently the opposite of weight in the content.

These four templates each fix that a different way, and they differ in one decision: how much of the page is visible before you ask for it. A. Shelf hides everything behind blocks you open. B. Manual keeps one index visible and one topic open. C. Hub answers the common questions on the surface and puts the long reads behind doors. D. Document shows everything at once, in reading order. The content is identical in all four; only the way in changes.

All four are built from css/ull-v2.css, the component layer the rest of the site was rebuilt on, so none of them introduces a new card, heading or button. Sections 3 and 4 do not exist yet; the copy for them is drafted in design/information/copy.md and the templates render that draft, not lorem.

Desktop only, as asked. The mobile route /mobile/info is untouched.

What is wrong with the page today

  1. 01The legends outweigh the guidelines. .info-cards is a 1fr 1fr grid and the Pending legend is given grid-column: 1 / -1 by an inline style, so nine icon rows occupy the full page width while the entire rulebook is read through a max-height: 600px pane below it.
  2. 02The guidelines scroll inside a page that scrolls. .info-guidelines-content is its own scroll container inside .info-page, so the wheel does one thing or the other depending on where the pointer is. Information.js carries an IntersectionObserver whose only job is to switch that inner overflow-y on and off — a workaround for a nesting that does not need to exist.
  3. 03The colouring legend redraws a component the site already has. Ten swatches are written as inline style="background:#5599ff", and those ten hex values are the same scale as .u-pill--blue … .u-pill--done in css/ull-v2.css. The legend for a colour system should be drawn with the colour system, or it will drift from it.
  4. 04Nothing tells a first-time visitor where anything is. The hero defines the list in four lines and the next thing on the page is a list of editors. There is no map of the site, and no answer to the questions that bring people here — how to submit, how points are earned — anywhere on it.
  5. 05The card hover from the pending page is here too. .info-guidelines:hover applies transform: scale(1.006), which nudges the layout under the pointer. The same tic was removed from the other pages in the last pass.

A. Shelf

blocks that open
js/pages/Information.js · css/pages/information.css

The page is a shelf of blocks sized by how much is behind them, and a block opens in place into the full width of the page. Closed, the whole page fits on one screen and every section states its size — “13 sections”, “14 questions”, “10 colours” — so you choose what to read before anything is read at you. Open, the block becomes the page: the guidelines get the full column width and one scroll, the two legends get a small panel, and nothing else moves except downwards. This is the block system you sketched, with the block sizes doing the arguing.

How it settles the weighting

Column span is the hierarchy and it is set once: the guidelines take eight of twelve columns, the FAQ four, the two legends two each under a “Reference” label. A legend can no longer look like a peer of the rulebook because it is a sixth of its size on the shelf, and opened it fills the same width as everything else, so nothing is privileged on the way in.

What it costs to build

The smallest of the four. One template, one stylesheet; each block is a native <details> so open/close, keyboard and Ctrl+F-into-a-closed-block are the browser's job, not state in Information.js. The guidelines keep their existing TOC markup, minus the fixed-height pane and the IntersectionObserver that patched it.

What to watch for

Closed blocks are a wall of invitations — someone who wants one fact still has to guess which block holds it, and a deep link has to open the right block on load. Two blocks open at once makes for a long page, so opening one may need to close the others, which is a decision to make before it is built.

Acceptance of Records

Requirements for proof of legitimacy

Before submitting a record, you must familiarise yourself with the list of mandatory proof of legitimacy, as well as the requirements for it, to ensure that the record you are submitting complies with them — otherwise it will be rejected immediately, before the verification stage.

1. Video recording of the record

The recording must show the attempt in full, from the starting percentage to the point of death or to 100%. It may contain editing elements intended to conceal personal information, in which case the original recording must be linked in the notes field.

  • the level must be the version listed on this site;
  • the endscreen must be visible, or the recording must continue past the completion;
  • cuts are permitted only where the raw footage is attached alongside.

2. Audio

In-game source audio or clicks. Either is accepted, both are strongly preferred; where neither is present, raw footage that has them must be attached.

Getting on the list
How does a level get on the list?

Staff select it. There is no queue a creator joins: moderators assess levels against the Level Selection criteria in the guidelines — classic gameplay, a public recording, an intended official-server release, and a credible chance of being rated — and place the ones that pass on the Pending List until an exact position is settled.

My level isn’t listed. Can I put it forward?

Raise it in #list-discussion with a public recording of its current state. It is not a submission form, and it goes through the same criteria as everything else.

Why is a level on Pending and not on the list?

It passed selection but has no exact position yet. Pending shows the range it is expected to land in and whether it is moving up or down inside it.

A level’s information is wrong.

Report it in #level-update-reporting with something to back it up — footage, a creator statement, tester feedback.

Records
How do I submit a record?

Through the Discord server. Read Acceptance of Records first — a record without the required proof is rejected before it is assessed, not sent back for more.

What proof does a record need?

A complete uncut playthrough (raw footage attached if your video has cuts), source audio or clicks, and a cheat indicator and fps/tps display where your mod menu provides them — on the version of the level this site lists.

What counts as a world record here?

Two things, tracked separately: the best completion from 0%, and the longest single run on the current version of the level.

How points are calculated

From two things only: the level’s rank in All Levels, and the percentage of the record. A verification is worth twice a 100% record on the same level; completing a level that isn’t verified yet is worth 0.8 of a verification.

Position is worth far more than percentage: a 50% on #1 beats a 100% on #100. And because points follow a level’s current rank, a total changes when levels move, without anyone submitting anything.

B. Manual

index + one reader
js/pages/Information.js · css/pages/information.css

The page stops being a stack of cards and becomes a small documentation site: one index on the left that lists everything on the page at the same grain — the two legends sit in it as two entries, the guidelines as thirteen — and one reader on the right that shows exactly one topic at a time, at full width. It is the layout the guidelines block already tries to be, promoted to run the whole page instead of being trapped in a 600 px window inside it. One search field covers the guidelines, the FAQ and the legends together.

How it settles the weighting

The index is the hierarchy, and it is honest by construction: an entry is one line whatever it holds, and the guidelines occupy thirteen of the twenty-one lines because there are thirteen of them. The legends stop competing on area — opened, every topic is the same reader, so the Pending legend gets a screen with nine rows on it and looks exactly as small as it is.

What it costs to build

The largest of the four, and the only one that needs real routing: every topic wants its own URL (/information/guidelines/cheating) so links into a rule survive, which means either hash routes or child routes under /information, plus a scroll-to-top on change and a mobile fallback since a two-pane layout has none.

What to watch for

A reader who does not already know what they want sees an index and nothing else — there is no front page, no first paragraph, and no way to skim the whole thing. It is also the least like the rest of the site: no other page here is a two-pane app, so /information would look like a different product unless the shell is kept familiar.

What this list is

A forecast of what the Demonlist is about to look like — not a record of what has already happened.

The Upcoming Levels List catalogues upcoming Top 1–100 Extreme Demons in Geometry Dash that are projected to be verified and placed on the Demonlist, along with unrated Extreme Demons that would have qualified for a rating when they were made.

Nothing here is official. Positions are estimates made by the list staff against written criteria, and they move as levels progress. Levels that are already rated are placed in strict accordance with their ranking on Pointercrate.

The three tiers

The order of levels is the same in all three. What changes is the threshold to appear at all.

  • All Levels — every level with a conceivable chance of being verified and published. A level’s rank here is what leaderboard points are calculated from.
  • Main List — levels that meet the standards required to be considered for an official rating.
  • Future List — levels very likely to be verified and published soon.

How to read a level

The colour of a name is the level’s state, from layout through decoration and verification to rated. Two percentages follow every level — how much of the decoration is done, and how far the best run has got. The badges are its position in each tier: a level can be #4 in All Levels and #2 in Future List at once.

C. Hub

answers up front, long reads behind doors
js/pages/Information.js · css/pages/information.css

The page answers first and files second. Above the fold: what the list is in three lines, and a map of the site — eight cards saying which page holds what, which is the thing a first-time visitor actually needs and the thing the page has never had. Below that, three doors sized by weight, and a reference strip. A door opens into a full-screen reader over the page, with the page still underneath, and closes back to exactly where you were. Nothing long is ever on the hub; the hub is a table of contents that happens to answer the easy questions itself.

How it settles the weighting

Weight is expressed twice over. On the hub the guidelines are a door twice the size of the others, carrying their five groups and a section count, while the two legends are a single quiet strip at the bottom under “Reference” — a strip, not a card, so they cannot read as sections. And a door opens to the full viewport, so the rulebook is finally read on a whole screen rather than in a window inside a card.

What it costs to build

Middling. The hub is static markup; the doors need one overlay component with a scroll lock, focus trap and Escape handling, reused three times. The map cards are the only genuinely new content, and they are eight lines of copy.

What to watch for

An overlay is a modal, and modals hide the page's depth: nothing on the hub tells you how long the guidelines are until you are inside them. It also needs to be deep-linkable (/information?open=guidelines) or a link to a rule cannot be shared, and history has to work — Back should close the door, not leave the page.

Acceptance of Records

Cheating and penalties

The list treats a record as a claim about what a player did, and the proof requirements exist so that claim can be checked. Where a record is found to have been obtained by prohibited means, the penalty depends on whether it was deliberate.

Unintentional violations

Where a prohibited modification was active but had no effect on the difficulty of the record, the record is rejected and the player is asked to resubmit without it. No further penalty follows a first occurrence.

Deliberate cheating

  • a submission ban, whose length depends on the severity and on previous occurrences;
  • removal of every record the player holds on this list;
  • a permanent Former cheater badge on the Discord server.

Submitting a record on behalf of another person — to evade a ban, to impersonate, or for no particular reason — bans both accounts for six months. Where two or more players share one account, each is banned for six months and the shared account is banned permanently.

D. Document

one page, one scroll
js/pages/Information.js · css/pages/information.css

Everything, visible, in reading order, on one scroll: what the list is, where everything lives, the FAQ, the two legends, the whole rulebook, then the staff. No block to open, no pane inside a pane, no modal — the only thing that scrolls is the page. A rail on the left is a mini-map rather than a menu: it shows where you are in the document and how much of it is left, and the guidelines nest under it as thirteen marks because they are thirteen marks of the total. This is the opposite bet to the shelf: that people skim, and that a page which hides itself cannot be skimmed.

How it settles the weighting

By length, which is the honest measure for a document: the guidelines are roughly three quarters of the page and the two legends are one compact band between the FAQ and the rules, set as a two-column reference strip rather than as sections. Nothing needs a size rule — a legend is short, so it looks short.

What it costs to build

Small, and the least risky. It deletes more than it adds: the nested scroll pane, the IntersectionObserver that manages it, and the card grid all go, leaving one column, anchors and a scroll-spy rail. Ctrl+F finds every rule on the page, every heading is linkable, and the page prints.

What to watch for

It is long — the guidelines alone are a substantial read, and after them the staff section is a long way down. It also gives the newcomer and the rules-lawyer the same page: whoever wants one fact scrolls past everything else to reach it, unless the rail carries the search field and does real work.

General

Introduction

These guidelines are directly adapted from, and heavily rely on, the structure and principles of the Global Demonlist Guidelines. We extend our full credit and gratitude to the original authors.

The text below explains how the list works in terms of accepting records, managing levels, recognising permitted and prohibited software, and establishing the duties and areas of responsibility of the list staff.

Glossary

The terms used throughout these guidelines and across the site — layout, decoration percentage, verification percentage, world record run, NC and NONG, and what “pending indefinitely” means.

Acceptance of Records

Definition of a World Record

Two records are tracked for every level. The world record from 0% is the highest completion percentage achieved from the start of the level. The world record run is the longest recorded segment performed on the current version of the level, measured as the difference between the percentage a run starts at and the point of death or completion.

Requirements for proof of legitimacy

Before submitting a record, you must familiarise yourself with the list of mandatory proof of legitimacy, as well as the requirements for it, to ensure that the record you are submitting complies with them — otherwise it will be rejected immediately, before the verification stage.

  • the recording must show the attempt in full, from its starting percentage to the point of death or to 100%;
  • it may contain editing intended to conceal personal information, provided the original recording is linked in the notes field;
  • in-game source audio or clicks — either is accepted, both are strongly preferred;
  • the level must be the version listed on this site.
