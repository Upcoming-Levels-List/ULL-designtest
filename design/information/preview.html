The mockups are live — click a block, hover a tile, close a window. Each is also a standalone page under pages/.

Design proposal

Three ways into one page

/information holds six unrelated things — what the list is, how to read it, the guidelines, two legends, who to talk to, and now the API — and today it stacks the four it has as cards of roughly equal size. The Pending List legend, nine rows of icons, is given a full-width card; the guidelines, thirteen sections across five groups, are given a 600 px window to be read through. Weight on the page is the opposite of weight in the content.

All three templates below open the same reader — one window, shared markup, the same typography — so what is actually being chosen between is the surface in front of it. A. Shelf is nothing but blocks, sized by what is behind them. C. Hub answers the easy questions on the page itself — the site map, and both legends in full — and puts the four long reads behind doors. E. Tiles shows names and sizes only, fills a tile in under the pointer, and opens it on click.

They are built from css/ull-v2.css, the component layer the rest of the site was rebuilt on, so none of them introduces a new card, heading or button. The copy for the three sections that did not exist — what the list is, the FAQ, the API — is drafted in design/information/copy.md, and the templates render that draft rather than lorem.

Scope
Desktop only, as asked. The mobile route /mobile/info is untouched.
Sections
The five agreed, plus API documentation. Submitting and points are FAQ entries; the glossary belongs to the guidelines.
New data
None. Editors come from data/_editors.json, guidelines from js/_guidelines.js, endpoints from worker/worker.js.
Dropped
B. Manual and D. Document, from the first round. They are in git history if either is wanted back.

What is wrong with the page today

  1. 01The legends outweigh the guidelines. .info-cards is a 1fr 1fr grid and the Pending legend is given grid-column: 1 / -1 by an inline style, so nine icon rows occupy the full page width while the entire rulebook is read through a max-height: 600px pane below it.
  2. 02The guidelines scroll inside a page that scrolls. .info-guidelines-content is its own scroll container inside .info-page, so the wheel does one thing or the other depending on where the pointer is. Information.js carries an IntersectionObserver whose only job is to switch that inner overflow-y on and off — a workaround for a nesting that does not need to exist.
  3. 03The colouring legend redraws a component the site already has. Ten swatches are written as inline style="background:#5599ff", and those ten hex values are the same scale as .u-pill--blue … .u-pill--done in css/ull-v2.css. The legend for a colour system should be drawn with the colour system, or it will drift from it.
  4. 04Nothing tells a first-time visitor where anything is. The hero defines the list in four lines and the next thing on the page is a list of editors. There is no map of the site, and no answer to the questions that bring people here — how to submit, how points are earned — anywhere on it.
  5. 05The card hover from the pending page is here too. .info-guidelines:hover applies transform: scale(1.006), which nudges the layout under the pointer. The same tic was removed from the other pages in the last pass.

A. Shelf

weighted blocks, popup reader
pages/a-shelf.html · js/pages/Information.js · css/pages/information.css

The whole page is a shelf of blocks sized by how much is behind them, and nothing else. Click one and it opens the reader over the page — the same window C and E open, so the three differ only in the surface in front of it. Closed, the seven blocks fit on one screen and each states its own size: “5 groups · 13 sections”, “14 questions”, “9 endpoints”, “10 colours”. You choose what to read before anything is read at you, and you come back to exactly the shelf you left.

How it settles the weighting

Column span, set once: the guidelines take eight of twelve, the FAQ four, the three middle blocks four each, and the two legends are small cards on a “Reference” row of their own beneath the shelf, sized to their contents. A legend cannot read as a peer of the rulebook when it is a sixth of its area and sitting under a different heading.

What it costs to build

Small. One grid, one modal component reused seven times, and the modal is where every long read lives — so the page itself never grows past one screen no matter how much the guidelines gain. The nested scroll pane and the IntersectionObserver that patches it both go.

What to watch for

Everything is behind a click, so a reader who does not know which block holds their answer has to guess, and search has to cover the closed blocks or it is decoration. A modal also needs a URL of its own (?open=api) or a rule cannot be linked, and Back must close it rather than leave the page.

Acceptance of Records

Requirements for proof of legitimacy

Before submitting a record, you must familiarise yourself with the list of mandatory proof of legitimacy, as well as the requirements for it, to ensure that the record you are submitting complies with them — otherwise it will be rejected immediately, before the verification stage.

1. Video recording of the record

The recording must show the attempt in full, from its starting percentage to the point of death or to 100%. It may contain editing elements intended to conceal the player’s personal information, in which case the original recording must be linked in the notes field.

  • the level must be the version listed on this site;
  • the endscreen must be visible, or the recording must continue past the completion;
  • cuts are permitted only where the raw footage is attached alongside.

2. Audio

In-game source audio or clicks. Either is accepted, both are strongly preferred; where neither is present, raw footage that has them must be attached.

3. Indicators

A cheat indicator, and an fps/tps display where the mod menu provides one. Neither is strictly mandatory, but a record without them is assessed more strictly and may be asked for raw footage.

Getting on the list
How does a level get on the list?

Staff select it. There is no queue a creator joins: moderators assess levels against the Level Selection criteria — classic gameplay, a public recording, an intended official-server release and a credible chance of being rated — and place the ones that pass on the Pending List until an exact position is settled.

My level isn’t listed. Can I put it forward?

Raise it in #list-discussion with a public recording of its current state. It is not a submission form, and it goes through the same criteria as everything else.

Why is a level on Pending and not on the list?

It passed selection but has no exact position yet. Pending shows the range it is expected to land in and whether it is moving up or down inside it.

A level’s information is wrong.

Report it in #level-update-reporting with something to back it up — footage, a creator statement, tester feedback.

Records
How do I submit a record?

Through the Discord server. Read Acceptance of Records first — a record without the required proof is rejected before it is assessed, not sent back for more.

What proof does a record need?

A complete uncut playthrough (raw footage attached if your video has cuts), source audio or clicks, and a cheat indicator and fps/tps display where your mod menu provides them — on the version of the level this site lists.

What counts as a world record here?

Two things, tracked separately: the best completion from 0%, and the longest single run on the current version of the level.

My record was rejected. Can it be reviewed?

Records rejected because the player changed the level are reviewable if the creator later made the same change in a new version. Anything rejected for missing proof needs resubmitting with the proof.

How points are calculated

From two things only: the level’s rank in All Levels, and the percentage of the record. A verification is worth twice a 100% record on the same level; completing a level that is not verified yet is worth 0.8 of a verification.

#1#10#50#100

50% record851698387248

Position is worth far more than percentage: a 50% on #1 beats a 100% on #100. And because points follow a level’s current rank, a total changes when levels move, without anyone submitting anything.

The Upcoming Levels List catalogues upcoming Top 1–100 Extreme Demons projected to be verified and placed on the Demonlist, along with unrated Extreme Demons that would have qualified for a rating when they were made. It is a forecast of what the Demonlist is about to look like — not a record of what has already happened.

Nothing here is official. Positions are estimates made by the list staff against written criteria, and they move as levels progress. Levels that are already rated are placed in strict accordance with their ranking on Pointercrate.

Where is what

All LevelsEvery level with a conceivable chance. A level’s rank here is what leaderboard points are calculated from. Main ListThe same order, higher bar: levels that meet the standards for an official rating. Future ListThe highest bar of the three — levels very likely to be verified and published soon. PendingAccepted but not yet placed, with the expected range and the direction it is moving. UpcomingThe same levels ordered by how close they are to verification rather than by difficulty. LeaderboardPlayers ranked by what their records, runs and verifications are worth. EventsLevel of the Day, Level of the Month, and the level closest to verification. A level’s pageStatus, progress, records, creators, and its rank in each tier.

How to read a level

The colour of a name is the level’s state, from layout through decoration and verification to rated. Two percentages follow every level — how much of the decoration is done, and how far the best run has got. The badges are its position in each tier: a level can be #4 in All Levels and #2 in Future List at once.

Everything this site shows comes from a public JSON API. It needs no key, no signup and no referrer, and it sends Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, so a page or a bot can read the list straight from the browser.

Base URL

https://d1-wrkr.ullteam.workers.dev

Endpoints

PathReturns
GET/api/listEvery level, in rank order
GET/api/list/mainThe Main List
GET/api/list/futureThe Future List
GET/api/levels/{position}One level, by its 1-based rank
GET/api/pendingPending List entries
GET/api/editorsThe staff list, in the order staff arranged it
GET/api/level-monthLevel of the Month, or null
GET/api/level-verifClosest to verification, or null
GET/api/recent-changesThe changes feed, grouped {date, entries[]}

Writing to the list needs a staff API key and is not part of the public API. There is no public leaderboard endpoint: the leaderboard is derived from /api/list, and the same rules are written out under “How points are calculated” in the FAQ.

Example

curl https://d1-wrkr.ullteam.workers.dev/api/list [ { "path": "manray", "name": "M A N R A Y", "author": "akunakunn", "creators": ["akunakunn", "Wobbly"], "verifier": "Open Verification", "percentFinished": 80, "records": [{ "user": "Zoink", "percent": 61, "hz": 240 }], "isMain": true, "isFuture": false, "isVerified": false, "sort_order": 4 }, … ]

The level object

FieldTypeMeaning
pathstringSlug; the level’s URL is /level/{path}
name, authorstringLevel name and host
creatorsstring[]Everyone credited
verifierstringVerifier, or "Open Verification"
percentFinishednumberDecoration progress, 0–100
records, runobject[]{user, link, percent, hz} — completions and runs
tagsstring[]Public, Layout, Rated, …
isMain, isFuture, isVerified, benchmarkbooleanWhich tiers it is on, and its state
sort_ordernumberRank in All Levels

Twelve further fields — id, rating, length, percentToQualify, lastUpd, the video URLs — are documented in full in the repository’s README.

Fair use

  • cache what you fetch: the list changes a few times a day, not a few times a second;
  • identify your bot in the user agent if you are polling on a schedule;
  • the data is community work — credit the list and link back to it;
  • positions are estimates and change, so treat a stored rank as a snapshot, not a fact.

C. Hub

answers up front, doors for the long reads
pages/c-hub.html · js/pages/Information.js · css/pages/information.css

The page answers first and files second. Above the fold: what the list is, and a map of the site — eight cards saying which page holds what, the thing a first-time visitor actually needs and the thing this page has never had. Under it, four doors sized by weight, and then the reference, in full, on the page itself: both legends together are nineteen short rows, which is less than the space an invitation to open them would take. Nothing long is ever on the hub; the hub answers the easy questions itself and opens the rest.

How it settles the weighting

Three ways at once. The guidelines are a door twice the width of the others and the full height of the column beside them; the FAQ, staff and API doors stack next to it as equals, because they are; and the two legends are not doors at all — they are printed at the bottom under “Reference”, in small type, where a lookup table belongs. Nothing that opens is a peer of the rulebook, and nothing that does not open pretends to be a section.

What it costs to build

Middling. The hub is static markup; the doors need one modal component with a scroll lock, focus trap and Escape handling, used four times. The map cards and the API block are the only genuinely new content.

What to watch for

A modal hides the page's depth — nothing on the hub says how long the guidelines are until you are inside them — and it needs a URL of its own (/information?open=api) or a rule cannot be linked. The hub is also the longest first screen of the three: map, doors and both legends before the fold on a short display.

E. Tiles

names at rest, detail on hover, everything on click
pages/e-tiles.html · js/pages/Information.js · css/pages/information.css

Three states instead of two. At rest a tile carries only its name and its size — “Guidelines, 13 sections”, “API, 9 endpoints” — so the whole page reads as a menu in one glance, with no paragraph competing with any other. Under the pointer the tile fills in: what is inside it, and a sample of the contents — the five guideline groups, the two questions people actually arrive with, the first endpoints. Clicked, it opens the same reader A and C open. A tile never changes size on hover and its name never shifts — the detail is printed into space the tile is already holding open beneath it — so nothing under the pointer moves.

How it settles the weighting

Area, and it is the loudest of the three: the guidelines tile is four times any other and holds the only large type on the page, the four working tiles are equal quarters, and the two reference tiles are short wide strips along the bottom that cannot be mistaken for sections. Because the resting state carries no prose, size is the only signal left, which is exactly what was wrong before — only now it is set deliberately.

What it costs to build

Small, and the same modal as A and C. The whole difference is one hover state per tile, in CSS. No new components, no routing beyond the one the modal needs anyway.

What to watch for

Hover is not available on touch and does not exist for a keyboard, so the resting state has to be sufficient on its own — the counts are doing real work, and the middle state must be treated as a bonus rather than as where the information lives. It is also the emptiest first screen: a visitor who wants to know what the list is sees seven names and no sentences until they move the mouse.

Position is worth far more than percentage: a 50% on #1 beats a 100% on #100. And because points follow a level’s current rank, a total changes when levels move, without anyone submitting anything.

Start hereWhat this list is

The Upcoming Levels List catalogues upcoming Top 1–100 Extreme Demons projected to be verified and placed on the Demonlist, along with unrated Extreme Demons that would have qualified for a rating when they were made. It is a forecast of what the Demonlist is about to look like — not a record of what has already happened.

Nothing here is official. Positions are estimates made by the list staff against written criteria, and they move as levels progress. Levels that are already rated are placed in strict accordance with their ranking on Pointercrate.

Where is what

All LevelsEvery level with a conceivable chance. A level’s rank here is what leaderboard points are calculated from. Main ListThe same order, higher bar: levels that meet the standards for an official rating. Future ListThe highest bar of the three — levels very likely to be verified and published soon. PendingAccepted but not yet placed, with the expected range and the direction it is moving. UpcomingThe same levels ordered by how close they are to verification rather than by difficulty. LeaderboardPlayers ranked by what their records, runs and verifications are worth. EventsLevel of the Day, Level of the Month, and the level closest to verification. A level’s pageStatus, progress, records, creators, and its rank in each tier.

How to read a level

The colour of a name is the level’s state, from layout through decoration and verification to rated. Two percentages follow every level — how much of the decoration is done, and how far the best run has got. The badges are its position in each tier: a level can be #4 in All Levels and #2 in Future List at once.

Search endpoints and fields…

Everything this site shows comes from a public JSON API. It needs no key, no signup and no referrer, and it sends Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, so a page or a bot can read the list straight from the browser.

Base URL

https://d1-wrkr.ullteam.workers.dev

Endpoints

PathReturns
GET/api/listEvery level, in rank order
GET/api/list/mainThe Main List
GET/api/list/futureThe Future List
GET/api/levels/{position}One level, by its 1-based rank
GET/api/pendingPending List entries
GET/api/editorsThe staff list, in the order staff arranged it
GET/api/level-monthLevel of the Month, or null
GET/api/level-verifClosest to verification, or null
GET/api/recent-changesThe changes feed, grouped {date, entries[]}

Writing to the list needs a staff API key and is not part of the public API. There is no public leaderboard endpoint: the leaderboard is derived from /api/list, and the same rules are written out under “How points are calculated” in the FAQ.

Example

curl https://d1-wrkr.ullteam.workers.dev/api/list [ { "path": "manray", "name": "M A N R A Y", "author": "akunakunn", "creators": ["akunakunn", "Wobbly"], "verifier": "Open Verification", "percentFinished": 80, "records": [{ "user": "Zoink", "percent": 61, "hz": 240 }], "isMain": true, "isFuture": false, "isVerified": false, "sort_order": 4 }, … ]

The level object

FieldTypeMeaning
pathstringSlug; the level’s URL is /level/{path}
name, authorstringLevel name and host
creatorsstring[]Everyone credited
verifierstringVerifier, or "Open Verification"
percentFinishednumberDecoration progress, 0–100
records, runobject[]{user, link, percent, hz} — completions and runs
tagsstring[]Public, Layout, Rated, …
isMain, isFuture, isVerified, benchmarkbooleanWhich tiers it is on, and its state
sort_ordernumberRank in All Levels

Twelve further fields — id, rating, length, percentToQualify, lastUpd, the video URLs — are documented in full in the repository’s README.

Fair use

  • cache what you fetch: the list changes a few times a day, not a few times a second;
  • identify your bot in the user agent if you are polling on a schedule;
  • the data is community work — credit the list and link back to it;
  • positions are estimates and change, so treat a stored rank as a snapshot, not a fact.
